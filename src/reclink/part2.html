Chapter 5

First power-up and the web configurator

Power the quad, join RecLink's Wi-Fi from your phone, and open the configurator. Everything from here on is done in that page.

[[shot name="first_screen" cap="The configurator on a phone." legend="1|RecLink logo. The centre dot is white with no camera, green with a camera connected, and flashes red while recording.;2|Seven tabs. Clock only appears in advanced mode.;3|Verdict banner: Ready, Not ready, or REC while recording.;4|Firmware version and uptime."]]
  1. Remove the propellers, then power the flight controller from a battery or USB. The RecLink LED lights solid while it boots.
  2. Wait for the portal. When the board sees the flight controller and the drone is disarmed, it starts its own Wi-Fi network.
  3. Join the network named RECLINK-XXXX (the last two bytes of the board's address) on your phone or laptop. The factory password is reclinkfpv.
  4. Open http://10.0.0.1 in a browser. Your phone may warn that the network has no internet. Stay connected. RecLink does not force a login sheet, so you open the address yourself.
  5. Change the Wi-Fi password when asked (below). Up to three devices can be connected at once.

Change the factory password

While the password is still the factory default, the configurator shows this prompt each time you open it. Choose Yes, change it, type a new password of 8 to 31 characters and press Save & reboot. The board restarts and the Wi-Fi comes back with your new password. Tick Don't ask me anymore if you want to keep the default; the choice is stored on the board.

Write it down

If you forget the password you can still reach the portal by holding the button for 10 seconds to factory reset. That erases all settings and camera pairings.

[[shot name="pw_modal" cap="First-run password prompt."]]

When the portal opens and closes

The portal never runs while the drone is armed.

  • Opens automatically when the drone is disarmed and the flight controller link is up. On the SuperMini and S3 boards you can change this to "only after holding the button" in Network → Open the portal. The RecLink board has no such option: its portal is always on while disarmed.
  • Closes after 15 minutes (adjustable from 1 to 120) once there has also been 60 seconds without activity, or when you hold the button for 3 seconds.
  • Never opens in flight. If the board reboots mid-flight, it waits until it knows the arm state before it will turn Wi-Fi on.

  • Nothing applies until you press Save settings. The bar sticks to the bottom of the screen. When you have unsaved edits it says "unsaved changes". If you try to leave the page with unsaved edits, the browser asks first.
  • Saves are permanent. They are written to flash before the page confirms. Settings and camera pairings survive a reboot, a power loss and a firmware update.
  • Advanced mode (System tab) reveals timing fields, diagnostics and the Clock tab. Leave it off while you set up; turn it on when you need to fine-tune. It is remembered on the board.
  • Two settings need a reboot: the flight controller baud rate / pin swap, and Bluetooth power. After saving one of them a Reboot now button appears in the bar.
  • The portal is a bench tool. The page polls the board every 1.5 seconds. Keep one browser tab open, and close the portal before flying.

Settings for each camera

The Cam control, OSD, Remote ID and Clock tabs hold settings that belong to one camera. Each paired camera keeps its own copy, and RecLink uses the copy of whichever camera is connected. Everything else (Wi-Fi, the flight controller link, LED, GPS push) is shared by all cameras.

[[shot name="camsel_locked" cap="Two cameras paired, none connected: the four tabs stay grayed out." legend="1|The Camera list at the top of those tabs, still on Select a camera."]]
[[shot name="camsel" cap="A camera chosen: its settings load and can be edited." legend="1|The chosen camera. The word in brackets says whether it is connected."]]
  • The page picks a camera for you. With one camera paired it selects that one. With several it selects the connected one, or else the one you used last on this phone. If none of those applies, the four tabs stay grayed out until you choose a camera in the Camera list.
  • It follows the camera that connects. When a camera connects, the list switches to it, unless you have unsaved edits. You can choose another paired camera in the list at any time. The page remembers your last choice in the browser.
  • Edit any paired camera, connected or not. Its settings are saved with it and apply as soon as it connects. The zone camera settings and Sync now need that camera to be connected.
  • A newly paired camera starts as a copy of the settings in use, so you only change what is different.
  • (connected) means the camera is linked now. (active) means RecLink is using that camera but it is still connecting.
  • With no camera paired, those tabs only ask you to pair one on the Binding tab.
If another camera takes over

If your usual camera is off, or another paired camera is closer, that camera connects and its settings take over right away, even in flight: its OSD lines, record mode, zones, Remote ID and clock. RecLink chooses by signal strength, not by which camera it used last.

Chapter 6

Pairing your camera

Pair once and RecLink reconnects by itself every time you power up. Pairing is done on the Binding tab.

GoPro

[[shot name="pair_modal" cap="Guided GoPro pairing." legend="1|Steps to put the GoPro into remote-pairing mode.;2|Optional GoPro Labs shortcut: point the camera at the code. Needs GoPro Labs firmware.;3|Cameras found appear here as buttons. Tap yours.;4|Start the search after the camera shows it is pairing."]]
  1. Open the Binding tab and press Pair GoPro.
  2. On the camera, swipe down, then go to Preferences → Connections → Connect Device → The Remote.
  3. When the camera shows that it is pairing, press Scan now in the portal. RecLink listens for up to 45 seconds. It does not connect to anything until you pick.
  4. Tap your camera in the list. If more than one GoPro is in pairing mode, check the name and signal strength first.
  5. A green check confirms the pairing. The camera appears under Paired cameras and its link goes to Connected.
  6. RecLink then asks you to name the camera. Type a name you will recognise, such as "Cinewhoop", and press Save name. You can press Skip and rename it later.
If no camera shows up

The portal says "No GoPro found. Make sure it is in remote-pairing mode, then press Scan now again". Check that the camera is awake and showing its pairing screen. If it is connected to a phone app, disconnect it there first.

DJI Osmo

[[shot name="pair_modal_dji" cap="Guided DJI pairing." legend="1|Steps to get the Osmo ready.;2|DJI cameras found appear here as buttons. Tap yours.;3|Start the search, then confirm the request on the camera's screen."]]
  1. On the Binding tab press Pair DJI. The same guided popup opens as for a GoPro.
  2. Switch the Osmo on, wake its screen and keep it within about a metre of the board. Close the DJI app on any phone: the camera accepts only one connection.
  3. Press Scan now and tap your camera in the list.
  4. Confirm the pairing request on the camera's screen. A first pairing waits up to 45 seconds for you to do this. A green check confirms the pairing.
  5. After this, reconnecting is automatic and needs no confirmation. As with a GoPro, RecLink then asks you to name the camera.

DJI support is still experimental. It has been bench-tested with one Osmo Action 6 and no other DJI camera: the camera connects, mode, resolution, frame rate, battery and SD space show up, and the 3 s test recording works. RecLink reads the model from the camera itself. It has not been flight tested.

An Osmo that is switched off

When the Osmo reports that it is switched off or asleep while its Bluetooth link is still up, RecLink drops the link at once and waits before it looks for that camera again: 5, then 10, 20 and 40 seconds. It does not chase a camera that is off, so another paired camera can connect. Switch the Osmo on and RecLink finds it again after the current wait. This wait has not been verified on hardware yet.

Managing paired cameras

[[shot name="binding" cap="Binding tab with two paired cameras." legend="1|Paired cameras with brand, address and state badge.;2|Pair another camera. The buttons disable at four.;3|Record for 3 seconds to prove the link works.;4|Forget one camera."]]
  • Up to four cameras, any mix of GoPro and DJI. Pairing a new one keeps the others.
  • One at a time. At power-up RecLink scans for every paired camera and connects to the strongest signal after about 2.5 seconds, or at once to one stronger than −50 dBm. A camera that fails to connect, or drops within a minute of connecting, is skipped for 30 seconds so another paired camera can connect.
  • Badges: ● Connected · −52 dBm, Connecting…, or Standby for a paired camera that is not in use.
  • 3 s test recording starts the connected camera for three seconds. Use it to prove the whole chain works. It only runs while disarmed.
  • Rename changes the name you gave a camera. The name is used in the Camera list, on this tab and on the Status tab. The camera's own name stays under it, next to its address.
  • Forget removes one camera. Forget all cameras appears when two or more are paired and asks twice.
  • Telemetry poll (advanced mode) sets how often a GoPro is asked for status: Fast 0.5 s, Normal 1 s or Slow 1.5 s. Normal is right for most setups.
When a bond is lost

If a GoPro forgets RecLink (you reset its connections, or re-paired it to a phone), the next reconnects fail. After three failures RecLink forgets that camera's bond and shows a Camera re-pair needed chip (REPAIR_NEEDED) on the Status tab. Pair the camera again. Nothing is forgotten while the drone may be airborne.

An Osmo is different. After it has been fully shut down for a while it forgets its pairing key and goes silent when RecLink uses the old one. RecLink notices at the first stall, removes its own stale key and pairs again by itself, which takes a few seconds, so you no longer need to re-bind. A bonded Osmo that drops the link at once while it is still starting up is not counted as a lost pairing. This was confirmed on the bench with one Osmo Action 6 only, and never in flight.

[[shot name="binding_empty" w="62mm" cap="A fresh board has no cameras. Pair one to begin."]]
The name is only a label

The name is stored on the RecLink board and used in this configurator. It is not written to the camera, and RecLink still finds and reconnects to the camera by its own name and address.

Chapter 7

Reading the Status tab

Status is read-only and tells you, at a glance, whether the chain flight controller → RecLink → camera is healthy. Read it from the top: the banner first, then the two tiles, then the camera card.

[[shot name="status_now" cap="Ready: the verdict banner and the two tiles." legend="1|Verdict banner. Green Ready, with the camera, its resolution and frame rate and the recording time left.;2|Aircraft tile: armed state, Betaflight variant, MSP version, OSD grid and GPS.;3|Camera tile: name, brand, link state and signal strength.;4|Signal bars."]] [[shot name="status_rec" w="62mm" cap="While recording the banner is red, with a pulsing dot and the elapsed time. The logo dot flashes red too."]]

The verdict

ReadyThe flight controller is linked, a camera is connected and nothing needs attention. The line under it shows the camera name, the resolution and frame rate and the recording time left.
REC 12:34The camera is recording and the timer shows how long. Any problem still appears as a chip under it.
Not readySomething needs attention. The problems are listed as chips under the heading. The banner is amber when there are only warnings and red when at least one problem needs action.

The two tiles

AircraftARMED in red, Disarmed in green, or No FC if the flight controller link is down. Under it: the Betaflight variant, the MSP API version, the OSD grid (the canvas size the FC reports) and the GPS fix with the satellite count, or "No GPS on the FC".
CameraThe camera name, its brand and link state (idle, connecting, bonding, syncing, lost) and, once connected, the signal in dBm with four signal bars. While the link is scanning it just says Scanning, and None if nothing is paired.
What Ready does not mean

Ready only describes the chain RecLink can see. It does not check your propellers, your flight battery or Betaflight's own arming checks.

Problem chips

[[shot name="status_repair" w="62mm" cap="Not ready: the problem is listed as a chip under the banner." legend="1|The banner turns red when a problem needs action.;2|One chip per problem. A chip with a cross is acknowledged by tapping it."]]

Every problem has its own chip, so you can see all of them at once. Amber chips are warnings. Red chips are problems that can cost you footage or need an action from you. Most chips disappear on their own as soon as the cause is gone.

Tap to acknowledge

The chip Recovered from a watchdog reset has a cross. It stays until you tap it, because the board restarted by itself and you should know.

Camera card

This card only exists once a camera is paired. It has four readouts and a list of settings.

  • Battery with a bar that turns red at 15 % or less, SD free in GB (or NO CARD / FULL), Time left for recording and Shooting: resolution, frame rate and aspect ratio.
  • Camera settings is a list that starts collapsed. For a GoPro it shows the profile (with bit depth), ISO max and min, shutter angle (for example 180° (1/120)), Protune, white balance, colour, sharpness, EV comp and 10-bit. A DJI does not report these values, so they stay hidden.
  • Zone settings, at the end of that list, is the result of the last zone apply: Zone 1: applied, "partly applied (not accepted: …)", "not applied" or "waiting for camera".
  • Test record (3 s) starts the connected camera for three seconds, the same as the button on the Binding tab. It is grayed out until a camera is connected, and it only works while the drone is disarmed.
  • Refresh asks the camera for fresh values.
A missing value is honest

RecLink only shows what the camera confirmed. A value the camera has not confirmed is hidden, not shown as a dash or a guess, and after a disconnect every camera value disappears until the camera answers again. That is deliberate: a stale or guessed number is worse than none.

With Advanced mode on, a Diagnostics card adds the live AUX channel values in µs, error counters and three collapsible debug panels (MSP link, Bluetooth connection, record trigger). They are useful for support, and for finding the stick position of a switch.

Set up the radio switch

[[shot name="cc_record" cap="Recording card with a radio switch mode selected." legend="1|The recording mode.;2|Switch (level) or Button (each press toggles).;3|Which AUX channel carries the control.;4|Detect the channel by moving the control.;5|Live position bar. The thin amber line is the control right now.;6|The switch counts as on above this value."]]
  1. Pick From a radio switch, Armed OR switch on or Armed AND switch on. The Radio switch box becomes active. With Never or When the drone is armed it is greyed out and marked "not applicable".
  2. Find the channel. Press Set AUX, then flip the switch on your transmitter. The label changes to "push button or switch" while it listens. RecLink watches the live channel data, picks whichever channel moves by 200 µs or more, and fills in the AUX field. It gives up after 15 seconds. This needs the flight controller link to be up.
  3. Choose Trigger type. Switch mirrors the switch: on means recording, off means stop. Button is for a spring-loaded control: each press toggles recording and releasing does nothing.
  4. Check the threshold. The bar shows the control's live position as an amber line. The default of 1700 µs suits most 3-position switches (the top position counts as on). Adjust it by dragging the handle or typing a value from 900 to 2100.
  5. Press Save settings and try the switch. The Status tab shows "REC" and the logo dot turns red.
Prefer to look at the numbers?

Network → Scan AUX now lists the live value of every AUX channel. Move your switch and press it again to see which channel changed.

What happens when you trigger it

Stopping works the same way after a stop delay (default 1.5 s), so the last moments are not clipped.

The camera is the judge: RecLink starts it, then waits for the camera to report it is recording. If it does not within the verify window, RecLink retries. If every try fails the OSD warning REC FAIL appears. The bridge tracks its own last command, so a camera that fails to report cannot cancel a stop RecLink owes it.
