Chapter 15

Remote ID: see other drones

RecLink listens for the Remote ID broadcasts of other drones nearby, and warns you on your OSD when one gets close, with its distance and direction. It is new in firmware 0.3.0 and it is off until you switch it on.

New, and not yet proven in flight

Remote ID is part of the 0.3.0 pre-release. It has not yet been validated in flight, nor for how it shares the radio with the camera link. Try it on the bench first, and watch your camera link closely the first times you fly with it.

Information, not collision avoidance

Many drones broadcast nothing, a Bluetooth receiver only hears Bluetooth transmitters, and range is limited. A quiet OSD never means the sky is clear. Keep your eyes on the sky.

What it does

Remote ID is the identity and position broadcast that many drones send while flying. RecLink receives those broadcasts, works out how far away each drone is and in which direction using the GPS of your own flight controller, and puts one short warning line on your OSD.

[[svg:rid_overview]]
  • Receive only. RecLink never transmits Remote ID. It is not a Remote ID module and does not make your own drone compliant with any regulation.
  • It uses the GPS you already have. There is no extra GPS to add. RecLink reads the position from your flight controller.
  • Off by default. Nothing changes until you switch it on in the web configurator.
  • Still a passenger. RecLink adds two reads from the flight controller, its heading and its altitude, and still only writes OSD text. Nothing it hears can reach arming, motors or the flight controller's own alarms.

What you need

When it runs

Remote ID runs only while it is switched on and the GPS is good. It waits 5 seconds of good GPS before starting, and it keeps going for 15 seconds after the GPS is lost, with a blank warning, so a brief dropout does not make the radios restart.

Inactive means nothing happens. No listening, no warnings and no failure message. Your OSD behaves exactly as it does without Remote ID. The Health card says why it is inactive.

While armed, the Wi-Fi radio is either off or only listening. RecLink sends nothing: no network, no connection, no scan, no frames. The configurator's own network still only exists while you are disarmed.

One radio, shared

The ESP32-C3 has one radio for Bluetooth and Wi-Fi, and the camera link must not suffer. How well Wi-Fi listening shares the radio with the camera link has not been proven yet. Start with Bluetooth, and move to Wi-Fi only if you need it and your camera link stays solid.

The Bluetooth scan effort setting is how much of the time RecLink listens. More listening finds drones sooner but takes more airtime from the camera link.

Reading the warning

The warning is one line of up to 16 characters.

[[svg:rid_osd]]

Distances are in metres up to 1 km, then kilometres. If your OSD uses imperial units (OSD tab, Units) they are in feet up to 1000 ft, then miles. The warning gets more urgent as the drone gets closer.

[[svg:rid_levels]]
LevelDefault distanceWhat you see
NoticeWithin 1000 mA steady !
WarnWithin 300 mThe ! blinks slowly, once a second
AlertWithin 100 mThe ! blinks fast, twice a second, and the warning takes the whole line
[[shot name="rid_warning" cap="The Proximity warning card." legend="1|Shows or hides the warnings. The receiver keeps running.;2|The distances for Notice, Warn and Alert. They change to feet when the OSD uses imperial units.;3|Bearing style: arrow, compass letters or degrees.;4|Blink the ! as the warning rises.;5|Also warn about drones that say they are on the ground.;6|A preview of each level with your settings, and of the failure text."]]
  • One drone at a time. If several are close, Alerts come first, one after another for 2 seconds each, each taking the whole line. Otherwise Warn and Notice messages take turns, highest level first, 2 seconds each, with your normal line shown for 2 seconds between rounds.
  • Above or below. A + after the direction means the drone is above you, a - below you. Within 15 m of your height it shows nothing.
  • Fast to rise, slow to fall. A level goes up at once, but an Alert needs two position updates inside the Alert distance, so one GPS glitch cannot raise it. A level only goes down when the drone is clearly farther than the limit.
  • Drones on the ground never go above Notice, unless you tick the box.
  • Old positions are dropped. If a drone's position is older than Position expires after (5 seconds by default), it disappears from the warning. RecLink never shows a stale distance, and never shows a made-up one.
  • Blinking needs a fast OSD. If your OSD refresh is too slow to show the blink, the ! stays steady instead. It needs at least 2 updates a second for Warn and 4 for Alert.
  • GPS is only accurate to a few metres on each side, so nothing finer than about 10 m is claimed. The Alert distance cannot be set below 20 m.

The direction arrow

The arrow shows where the other drones is relative to your nose, in the same way as Betaflight's home arrow: up means straight ahead. For that RecLink needs to know which way your drone is facing.

[[svg:rid_heading]]

This works with a GPS only, with or without a compass. Once you have flown at 5 m/s or faster for 5 seconds with 5 or more satellites, RecLink knows its heading well enough to draw the arrow. Until then, it shows compass letters (N, NE, E and so on) instead. A compass does not shorten the wait, because RecLink cannot tell whether the flight controller is actually using it. The Heading row in Health shows whether the heading comes from the FC compass or is GPS-aligned, or is none yet.

When the receiver fails

If Remote ID is active and a way of listening that you switched on is not working, the warning line shows RID RX FAIL instead of staying quiet. It blinks for 5 seconds, then stays steady. This is so that a dead receiver is never mistaken for an empty sky.

  • It appears when the Bluetooth scan is not running, when Wi-Fi listening did not start while armed, or when the Remote ID part itself stopped responding. The Faults row in Health names which one.
  • An Alert from a path that still works outranks the failure text, and the failure text outranks Warn and Notice.
  • A missing GPS is not a failure: the feature is just inactive and shows nothing.
  • If the Remote ID part stops responding, RecLink switches only that feature off until the next reboot. It does not restart the bridge in flight, so your camera control and OSD keep working.

Your own drones

If your own drone carries a Remote ID module, RecLink would warn you about it. Add its ID to My drones (up to four). A new drone whose ID has not been heard yet can show at most a Notice for its first 15 seconds, so your own module cannot raise an Alert before the list has a chance to match it.

Test the reception

Test reception (30 s) listens for 30 seconds and lists every drone it hears. It works indoors with no GPS. Without a GPS fix it shows the ID and source only, with no range or direction.

  • It only runs while disarmed, and only with the receiver on and saved. Arming stops it at once.
  • With Bluetooth only, the configurator stays connected.
  • If the mode includes Wi-Fi, RecLink asks first: the board's Wi-Fi drops for about 30 seconds and your phone disconnects. Reconnect after about 40 seconds and open the page again.
  • Use a drone with Remote ID turned on, or a Remote ID phone app, close by.

Privacy and rules

  • RecLink keeps only a drone's ID, range, direction and whether it is above or below you. Everything else in the broadcast, such as the operator's ID, free text and location, is thrown away.
  • What it keeps lives in memory only. It is never written to flash and never appears in the log.
  • Data leaves the board only through the Remote ID tab, while the configurator is open.
  • RecLink makes no claim about regulation. Whether receiving or showing other people's Remote ID data is restricted where you fly has not been researched, so check your local rules.

What RecLink can and cannot touch

It reads

  • Flight controller: API version, craft name, arm state, RC channels, GPS, battery state, clock.
  • Camera: recording, battery, SD, mode, resolution, frame rate and the other values shown on the Status tab.

It writes

  • Four lines of OSD text to the flight controller.
  • Record, settings, highlight and clock commands to the camera.

It never sends

  • Any RC, GPS, clock, display-port or reboot command to the flight controller.
  • Any command that arms or disarms.
  • Any formatting, firmware, reset or file-delete command to the camera from automation. Those exist only as labelled portal actions, while disarmed.
